# Workflow examples

## Sufficient setup request

Learner: “Help me learn Spanish so I can text my partner's family. I know basic Spanish and have two hours a week.”

Do not ask dialect, activity, correction-style, or scheduling questions. Generate and validate the campaign, persist it once, give a two-sentence overview, and begin the first practical texting mission.

## Casual goal

Learner: “I mainly want to understand Portuguese tweets.”

Treat reading social posts as the practical outcome. Ask only weekly time and prior knowledge if absent; do not replace it with a traditional course goal.

## New goal

Learner with active Spanish family-texting campaign: “Now I need French for a job interview.”

This changes target language and real-world outcome. Transition to a new campaign; preserve the Spanish campaign as paused. Do not edit its goal or discard its evidence.

## Accidental help

After announcing a check, Codex supplies the needed conjugation. Do not score the learner response. Call `return-to-practice`, explain briefly that the check will be retried independently later, and continue guided practice with refreshed content.

## Ambiguous resume

If two paused Spanish campaigns plausibly match “continue Spanish,” show their sanitized goals as a numbered choice. Do not choose based on recency alone.
